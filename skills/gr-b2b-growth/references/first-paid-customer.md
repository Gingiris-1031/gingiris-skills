# Diagnose the first paid-customer bottleneck

Use when a team already has interested prospects but keeps adding names instead of closing the first usable, paid workflow. Do not use an arbitrary list size as the stop rule.

For each relevant opportunity, check the buyer and urgent job, demonstrated interest, minimum deliverable, current workflow failure, offer clarity and payment readiness. If known opportunities are blocked by the same product or commercial issue, pause additional prospecting for that segment and assign the blocker an owner. If there is no credible buyer/problem evidence, continue discovery rather than forcing a sale.

Output:

`Opportunity stage (anonymized) | Buyer / job evidence | Main blocker | Smallest next action | Owner / date | Proof required to advance | Prospecting decision`

Distinguish interest, a trial, a signed agreement and received payment. A design partner or prepayment is not proof of a repeatable sales motion. State what can actually be delivered before accepting an offer; choose a supported payment method only after confirming account, currency and reconciliation requirements.

## Pricing promise → product acceptance

Before recommending a pricing-page release, fill:

`Public promise | Buyer-understandable unit | Included usage | Overage rule | Implemented capability | Test evidence | Owner | Publish / revise / defer`

Check units together: concurrency, seats, minutes and credits should fit the intended job, not merely copy a competitor. Explain what happens when the allowance is exceeded. A promised feature must work in the relevant tier; omit or clearly label unavailable features. A few positive price reactions are interview evidence, not market-wide willingness to pay.

Finish with the week's main bottleneck, the activity to pause, the customer milestone to pursue and the evidence that would justify resuming acquisition. Link to deal-execution.md only when contract or delivery mechanics are actually the blocker.
