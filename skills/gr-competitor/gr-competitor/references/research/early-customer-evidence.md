# Early customer evidence → ICP hypotheses

Use when “power users” or a broad product category fails to explain who buys and why. Start with a small set of relevant competitors; prefer dated first-party customer stories and archived early pages. A target such as the first ten paying customers is a research question, not a promise that those records exist.

Record:

`Source URL | Publication / archive date | Evidence of early status | Evidence of payment | Industry | User role / buyer | Job and trigger | Previous workaround | Outcome stated by source | Unknowns`

A logo, testimonial or case study alone does not establish payment or early-customer status. Label those unknown explicitly. Keep source claims, customer statements and your inference separate. Do not infer a full customer list from a few public examples or collect private identities to fill gaps.

Group observed jobs into one to three industry hypotheses, with disconfirming evidence and questions for interviews. “Consultants preparing a client deliverable from scattered notes” is a testable job; “AI power users” alone is not. Competitor evidence can guide recruitment but cannot validate your own PMF.

## Handoff from complaint to a testable change

A complaint describes a problem, not the solution. When product recommendations are requested, add:

`Evidence | User task | Current failure | Proposed solution | Reference implementation / benchmark | Category | Priority rationale | Owner | Acceptance steps | User to revisit`

Categories may be core capability, onboarding or commercial readiness. Select P0/P1/P2 from impact and dependencies; do not label every complaint P0. Describe the starting state, user action and observable result for acceptance. Mark a solution as proposed until tested. If interviews are available, use gr-user-interview to validate; otherwise include the unanswered interview questions in the handoff.
